# Role angles — how different designations use this project

The data, the flow, and the limits are the same in every angle. What changes is which part of the project each role leads with, and what question they answer in an interview.

## Associate / Assistant Product Manager (APM)

An APM improves a business by understanding the problem before building the solution. Lead with:

- **Discovery:** the intake analysis — what requests actually were, what information was missing, and what problem each request solved.
- **User journey:** the value-stream map as a journey, from request to release, with waiting time as the user's experience of delay.
- **Prioritisation:** the P1/P2/P3 tiers and the impact vs. effort matrix, and the trade-off made visible when low-impact work was deferred.
- **Metrics:** lead time, throughput, rework, and escapes as product health metrics, not vanity metrics.
- **Experiment:** the phased plan as an experiment — one change per cause, measured against baseline, kept or killed on evidence.

What to say in an interview: how you would decide what to build first, how you would know if it worked, and what you would stop doing. That is the APM story in this project.

## Product Manager (PM)

Lead with scope, roadmap, and launch: the charter, the scope in and out, the trade-offs in prioritisation, the go / no-go at release, and the launch checklist. A PM owns the decision, not just the flow.

## Product Operations

Lead with the system that makes product work repeatable: intake, playbook adoption, WIP limits, reporting routines, and the daily flow review. Product Operations is the role that turns a product decision into a workflow other teams can follow.

## Business Operations

Lead with capacity and predictability: demand vs. throughput, backlog arithmetic, lead time range, the monthly business review, and the business-model document. Business Operations answers: can we serve this demand, at this speed, with this team, and what happens if demand rises?

## Process Improvement / Continuous Improvement

Lead with method: value-stream mapping, waste (waiting, rework, overprocessing), Pareto, phased interventions, and the control plan with triggers. This is the Lean and DMAIC story, with numbers that show the method working, and limits stated for what four control weeks can and cannot prove.

## Customer Experience

Lead with the customer side of flow: waiting time as customer waiting, rework as a customer contacting again or living with a defect, escapes as a customer finding the problem after release, and recovery as a runbook. The flow improvement is, from this angle, a customer-effort improvement.

## Across all roles

The transferable core is the same, and it is what makes one project usable for many designations: understand the flow, measure it cleanly, find the bottleneck with data, change one thing per cause, and control the result with a routine. An APM does it with discovery and prioritisation, a PM does it with scope and launch, a Business Operations manager does it with capacity and forecasting, and a Process Improvement manager does it with mapping and control. Same pipeline, different lens.
