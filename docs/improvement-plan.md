# Improvement plan — one change per cause, in phases

## Phase 1 — Baseline, Weeks 1–4

Measure only. No changes. The as-is value stream, the Pareto, and the weekly table are built from baseline data, so the improvement can be checked against a real starting point.

## Phase 2 — Improve, Weeks 5–8

One intervention per cause, in Pareto order:

- **Week 5 — Intake:** completeness checklist and intake gate, plus priority tiers P1/P2/P3. Addresses incomplete intake, 32%.
- **Week 6 — Ownership and WIP:** one named owner per stage, WIP limits, and the daily flow review. Addresses unclear ownership, 24%, and excess WIP, 13%.
- **Week 7 — Early quality:** quality gate at scoping and mid-build check, in addition to final review. Addresses late quality discovery, 21%.
- **Week 8 — Release discipline:** release checklist, freeze window, and rollback plan. Addresses last-minute release changes, 10%.

## Phase 3 — Control, Weeks 9–12

No new changes. The same KPI definitions, the same WIP limits, the same playbook, and the same daily and weekly reviews. Control is a test: does the improvement hold when no one is actively changing the process?

Control triggers: if lead time rises above 14 days or rework rises above 10% for two consecutive weeks, a root-cause review is opened, and the playbook is checked before any new change is proposed.

## Why phased, not all at once

Changing everything at once would make it impossible to say what worked. Phasing by Pareto order also means the largest causes are fixed first, so the early weeks show whether the approach is working at all, before smaller causes are touched.
