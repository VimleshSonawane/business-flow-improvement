# Intake and prioritisation

Intake is where this pipeline was won or lost. A request that enters incomplete creates delay in every later stage, so intake is treated as a gate, not a mailbox.

## Intake form — required before a request is accepted

- What is being requested, in one sentence
- Who it is for: member, guest, partner, or internal team
- What problem it solves, and what happens if it is not done
- Scope: what is in, what is out
- Deadline or event date, if any, and why that date matters
- Owner on the requesting side, and approver if approval is needed
- Dependencies: content, asset, vendor, or system needed before build can start

A request missing any of these is returned to the requester with the missing items named. It is not accepted and queued in the hope that the information appears later. That rule alone addresses the largest Pareto cause, incomplete intake at 32%.

## Priority tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Member or guest impact now, or a dated event that cannot move | Triaged same day, enters scoping first |
| P2 | Planned improvement with a clear benefit and a known date | Triaged within 2 days, ordered by impact and effort |
| P3 | Nice to have, no date, benefit not yet clear | Held in a backlog review, not started until P1 and P2 are flowing |

Priority is decided at triage, by the pipeline lead with the stage owner, not by who shouts loudest and not by arrival order alone.

## Prioritisation matrix

Impact is judged on reach, member or guest value, and risk if delayed. Effort is judged on build time, dependencies, and quality risk. High impact and low effort goes first. High impact and high effort is scoped carefully before it enters build. Low impact, whatever the effort, waits.

## Triage rule

- Every accepted request gets a named owner, a tier, and a definition of done before it leaves intake.
- No request enters scoping without an owner.
- Intake WIP limit is 10. When intake is full, the triage conversation is about what to defer, not about adding a second queue.
