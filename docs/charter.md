# Charter — Business Flow Improvement

## Problem

A simulated pipeline for a subscription product and live experience operation was completing about 28 requests a week against demand of about 35 a week. Lead time was about 21 days, rework was 22%, and quality escapes were 14%. Backlog grew from 84 to 110 in four baseline weeks.

The problem was flow, not effort: incomplete intake, unclear ownership, too much work in progress, late quality checks, and last-minute release changes.

## Goal

Improve the flow of the pipeline so that requests move faster, with less rework and fewer escapes, without adding headcount in the simulated case.

## Targets, judged on the control phase (Weeks 9–12)

- Lead time: under 14 days (baseline about 21)
- Throughput: at or above demand, about 35 a week (baseline about 28)
- Rework: 10% or lower (baseline 22%)
- Quality escapes: 6% or lower (baseline 14%)
- Waiting time: under half of lead time (baseline about 15 of 21 days)

All five targets are met in the control phase in the simulated case.

## Scope

In scope: intake, triage, scoping, build and preparation, quality review, release and delivery, and post-release review. Also in scope: WIP limits, definitions of done, playbooks, reporting, and the control plan.

Out of scope: hiring and headcount changes, new tooling purchases, financial accounting, physical safety operations, and content production craft.

## Stakeholders

- **Requesters:** product, content, experience, and partner teams who submit requests
- **Pipeline lead:** owns the flow, the daily review, and the control plan
- **Stage owners:** one named owner per stage, owns work in that stage and its definition of done
- **Quality reviewer:** owns the quality gates and the escape log
- **Operations lead:** owns capacity, WIP limits, and the weekly business review
- **Sponsor:** approves scope changes and the move from Improve to Control

## Success criteria

Success is not one good week. It is four control weeks, with the same KPI definitions throughout, a fixed rework and escape rule, and a review trigger if lead time rises above 14 days or rework rises above 10% for two consecutive weeks.
