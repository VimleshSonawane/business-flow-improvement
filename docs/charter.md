# Project Charter — Approval Workflow Improvement (Domestic Expense Declarations)

**Project type:** Self-initiated portfolio project. Real public data (BPI Challenge 2020, a university's travel-expense approval process, 2017–2018). Stakeholder roles are role-played.
**Project manager and analyst:** Vimlesh Sonawane
**Charter date:** 2026-10-30

## Background
Employees submit domestic travel-expense claims, which pass through administration, budget owner and supervisor approval before payment.

## Problem statement (2018 baseline, from docs/baseline-kpi-report.md)
- Paid claims take a median of 8.05 days from submission to payment, but 1 in 10 takes more than 20.25 days (7,903 paid cases).
- 13.14% of submitted claims are rejected at least once (1,072 of 8,160). Administration makes 948 of the 1,229 rejections.
- Rejected claims take 1.8 times as long to pay (median 13.21 vs 7.34 days).
- Payments are released in batches: 99.66% fall on a Monday or Thursday, and the wait before payment (median 3.21 days) is the longest median wait of any step.

## Goal and proposed targets (to be confirmed after root-cause analysis)
- Reduce P90 cycle time for paid claims from 20.25 days to 14 days or less.
- Reduce the share of submitted claims rejected at least once from 13.14% to 8% or less.
- Reduce the median wait before payment from 3.21 days to 1 day or less.

## Scope
In: domestic declarations; 2018 as the main view, 2017 as reference only; all steps from submission to payment; rejections and rework; control exceptions.
Out: international declarations, travel permits, prepaid travel costs and requests for payment; implementing system changes; staffing and budget decisions.

## Risks

- No access to real process owners — label causes as hypotheses until tested in the data.
- Scope creep into the other four logs — keep them on the out-of-scope list.
- 2017 covers only two departments — use 2018 as the main view; never show 2017 vs 2018 as a trend.
